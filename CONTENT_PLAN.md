Status: **approved at Checkpoint A** (2026-10-18). Being built in the order of section 7.
The slice (ballroom, study, kitchen, balcony; 12 people; 3 Contracts) stays as
it is and becomes the first act.

## 1. The mystery: who keeps resetting midnight?

**Setting.** Ashcombe Hall, New Year's Eve 1929, two months after the Wall
Street Crash. Everyone at the party is quietly ruined, or about to be.

**The answer: Hobbs, the butler.**

- Lord Ashcombe lost the Hall to Silas Calloway at cards in the cellar. The
  deed changes hands **at the stroke of midnight**.
- Hobbs has served the house for 51 years. "A butler does not permit the
  year to end in disgrace." He found the old astronomical clock in the clock
  tower that winds back the evening, and he rewinds it at 11:59 every night.
- He has done this **4,011 times** and can't fix the evening alone. So he
  hired outside help: **you**. Most of your "clients" (the nervous
  shareholder, the unsigned telegrams) were Hobbs, typing in the pantry.
- You remember each loop because of the **silver pocket watch engraved "H."**
  that you start with. It's wound in step with the tower clock. The first
  clue is in your hand from minute one.

**How it comes out (through the intel board, a new "Who keeps time?" column):**

| Clue | Where it comes from | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| The pocket watch "H." | Start of the game | Someone gave you this. Who? |
| Same typewriter | Telegrams + the butler's pantry | All your clients used one machine |
| Hale's tally marks | Contract 4 | The inspector half-remembers. 4,011 marks |
| The "Chrono-Toaster" is a fake | Contract 5 | Rules out the obvious suspect, Dr Faraday |
| The deed and the midnight clause | Contract 6 | Why midnight matters |
| "The one who keeps the time" | Contract 7 (séance) | The spirit is Hobbs on the speaking tube |
| Calloway is broke | Contract 1 (ledger) | The deed is worthless anyway |
| Vivienne's trust | Contract 3 + talk | The house was never really at stake |
| Fresh oil and white gloves | Clock tower | Hobbs, caught in the act |

**The one ending: "Let Midnight Strike."** It's the final Contract. When the
board is complete, you show Hobbs that the house is safe, or you simply stop
him. Midnight strikes, it's 1930, and fireworks go up. A short epilogue gives
each guest a dry closing line that reflects what you did. The last line goes
to Hobbs: *"Nineteen-thirty, sir. I have a feeling it will be a quiet decade."*

**Red herrings:** Dr Faraday's time machine (it's a toaster), Madame Zelda's
séances (a fraud, terrified that her predictions keep coming true), and
Inspector Hale (he's counting the loops too, but he's a victim, not the cause).

## 2. Contracts (9 in total, easiest first)

Every Contract has a goal, a twist and four approaches. Being spotted never
fails a Contract outright. It starts a chase or a brawl.

| # | Contract | Goal | Twist | Quiet | Social | Loud | Chaos |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Last Song** *(built)* | Dolly must not sing at 10 | Vivienne isn't jealous. She's saving Dolly from Calloway's "contract" | Cut the mic cable | The "picture man" lure | Start a brawl | Blackout |
| 2 | **The Ledger** *(built)* | Get Calloway's ledger | The ledger shows the bank is bust (story clue) | Crack the safe | Blackmail (the affair) | Knock him flat at 11 | Grab it in the dark |
| 3 | **The Midnight Toast** *(built)* | Stop Ashcombe's toast | The toast confesses he lost the Hall | Spiked vintage, waiter disguise | Tell Vivienne | Balcony knockout | Drop the chandelier |
| 4 | **The Inspector's Notebook** | Get Hale's notebook away from him | It isn't evidence. It's 4,011 tally marks | Lift it while he dozes by the library fire (8:40) | Hand him a bigger case (the cellar casino) and he drops Rupert | Knock him into the conservatory fishpond | Ring the fire bell. The library empties and the notebook is left on the chair |
| 5 | **The Chrono-Toaster** | Faraday's 11:15 demo must fail | It was always going to fail. She came to swindle Calloway | Swap a valve in her crate in the garage | Show her Calloway is broke, so she cancels | Tackle the machine off its plinth | Crank the boiler. Steam pipes burst in the conservatory |
| 6 | **House Rules** | Win the deed back from the cellar card game | The dealer cheats for Calloway, and Hobbs witnessed the deed | Dress as a croupier and swap the deck on the dealer's break | Expose the cheat with intel, so Calloway forfeits | Flip the table and run with the deed | Open the champagne store's pressure valve. | 7 | **The Séance** | Make the "spirits" name Rupert heir | A real voice answers: Hobbs, through the speaking tube | Take over the speaking tube before Hobbs does | Blackmail Zelda with her trick box | Grab her trumpet and announce it yourself | Blackout, then "levitate" the table with the dumbwaiter rope |
| 8 | **Cold Feet** | Pierre must propose to Elsie at the boathouse (11:30) and she must say yes | Brock, the muscle, is Elsie's big brother | Fish the ring out of the frozen fountain, light the lanterns, get the timing right | Win Brock over (he cries at weddings) | Keep Brock busy with a snowball brawl | Set off Old Tom's fireworks early as the "moment" |

- **No stealth needed:** every Contract has Social, Loud and Chaos routes that
  work even if you've been seen all evening. The bot test plays all 36 routes,
  and every Loud route is tested with the player already spotted.

haven't loaded yet stay "locked by a guest" for a moment and are never shown
broken.

## 4. Recurring characters (8 leads) and running gags

| Who | Personality | Running gag |
|---|---|---|
| **Hobbs**, the butler | Perfect, unflappable, quietly exhausted | Knows things he shouldn't: "Your usual, sir? ...That is, a drink, sir." The slips get bigger each loop. |
| **Lord Ashcombe** | Bluster, borrowed confidence | Rehearses his toast all evening and fluffs the same line every time |
| **Lady Vivienne** | Dry, shrewd, holds the real money | Corrects everyone's facts, including yours, accurately |
| **Silas Calloway** | Banker, pale, sweating | Reads ruin in everything. Pulls ticker tape from his pockets like a magician |
| **Dolly Vane** | Singer, irrepressible | "Future star of the talkies." Her high note cracks one particular glass, always the same one |
| **Rupert Ashcombe** | Nephew, schemer | Has a new get-rich-quick scheme every hour, and asks you to invest |
| **Inspector Hale** | "Off duty" | Gets a stronger sense of déjà vu each loop: "Have we... no. Carry on." |
| **Dr Ottoline Faraday** *(new)* | Inventor, magnificent fraud | Every invention turns out to be a toaster |

**Supporting cast:** Mrs Pryce (gossip columnist, always one scoop behind),
Pierre and Elsie (the proposal), Chef Marcel (everything is "a
catastrophe"), Brock (always gives chase, always runs out of puff on the
stairs), plus two new people: **Madame Zelda** (a medium, panicking because
she's right) and **Old Tom** (groundskeeper and fireworks man, hard of hearing).
That makes 15 people, built with the same outfit generator as the slice.

## 5. Meta progression: perk tree

Memory points come from Contracts, first-time intel and secrets. Perks only
make things easier and add flavour. **Nothing is ever locked behind a perk.**

| Branch | Tier 1 | Tier 2 | Tier 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Timekeeper** | Quick Feet *(built)*: sprint +25% | Déjà Vu: people's next destinations show as faint trails for 10 s | The Watch Winds Back: once per loop, rewind 10 minutes |
| **Quiet** | Butler's Spare Key *(built)* | Wardrobe: start in the waiter's jacket | Light Step: you're noticed from a shorter distance |
| **Social** | Good Memory: talk options show what a clue will unlock | Name-Dropper: one free secret per loop | Silver Tongue: retry a failed persuasion once |
| **Loud** | Iron Jaw: take one extra punch | Bad Knee: chasers tire 30% faster | Rugby Tackle: knock someone down from a sprint |
| **Chaos** | Sparky: trip the fuses from any speaking tube | Butterfingers: dropped items cause bigger distractions | Pyrotechnician: fire Old Tom's rockets remotely |

15 perks in total. Tier 2 needs one perk from tier 1 in the same branch.
Tier 3 needs one from tier 2.

## 6. Secrets worth finding (10)

1. **Hobbs's pantry diary**, a page per loop. The funniest reading in the game (also a story clue).
2. **The speaking tubes**: brass pipes linking six rooms. Listen in, or throw your voice.
3. **The casino door** behind the third wine rack (pull the 1888 port).
4. **The library bookcase passage** up to the Long Gallery.
5. **The portrait with peephole eyes**: spy on the gallery from the servants' stair.
6. **Mister Biscuit, the manor cat**: in a different room each loop. Find all 8 spots for a perk point bonus.
7. **Rupert's car keys**: a short, silly drive round the snowy grounds.
8. **Skates in the boathouse**: skate on the frozen lake (a fast shortcut, and fun).
9. **"The Big One"**: Old Tom's forbidden rocket, hidden in the shed (needed for one finale route).
10. **The toaster works**: use Faraday's machine and it makes perfect toast. Hale is moved.

## 7. Build order (after approval)

1. **Background streaming** (so the game starts fast as the manor grows), then
   the **Grand Foyer + Library** with **Contract 4**. This is **Checkpoint B**.
2. Conservatory and Garage → Contract 5. Cellar (wine, casino, boiler) →
   Contract 6. Upper floor (gallery, suite, billiard room) → Contract 7.
   Grounds → Contract 8. Clock tower → Contract 9. Screenshots checked for
   every room. Bot plays every route.
3. Story column on the intel board, plus banter that changes as clues are found.
4. Perk tree, secrets, ending and epilogue.
5. Challenge modes: Speed Loop, No Alarms, Chaos Only, Daily Seed (a seeded
   twist each day: which room the cat is in, a reshuffled guest, a locked door).
6. Audio: music that shifts with the hour and with tension, ambient sound per
   room, short synthesised "barks".
7. Settings and accessibility: remappable keys, subtitles,
   colourblind-safe icons (shape plus colour), reduced motion, touch sizes.
8. Polish, then a first-minute review as a brand-new player. This is **Checkpoint C**,
   then **Checkpoint D**.

**Honest size note:** 12 new rooms plus 6 Contracts is about four times the
slice. Rooms reuse the modular kit, and props come from CC0 libraries (Poly Haven,
Quaternius, Kenney). The tower gears, the casino and the fireworks are built
procedurally. If an area can't reach the slice's visual bar, I'll flag it at
Checkpoint C rather than ship it rough.
